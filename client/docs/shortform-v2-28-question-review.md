# KnowYouRole Short-Form V2 — 28-Question Review Draft

Status: Review draft only. Not wired into local dev server.

## Design target

- 28 fixed core questions.
- Mostly 4-option forced-choice tradeoffs.
- Each answer is positive; no option is framed as the “bad” answer.
- Each question produces multiple signals: Big Five, MBTI-style axis, DISC work behavior, career-fit vector, and sometimes confidence/consistency.
- Designed to improve role-result resonance, not merely personality-label entertainment.

## Signal groups

1. MBTI-style identity layer: E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P.
2. Big Five backbone: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Emotional Stability / Stress Reactivity.
3. DISC work behavior: D, I, S, C.
4. Career-fit vector: analysis, systems, people, leadership, operations, creative, hands-on, service, entrepreneurship, stability, autonomy, pace.
5. Calibration / confidence: checks whether the user’s stated strengths, pressure style, and work-energy choices agree with earlier signals.

## Group A — Core operating style / MBTI-style axes

### Q01 — Recharge pattern
**Purpose:** E/I, Extraversion, preferred work rhythm.

Prompt: After a demanding day, what most reliably helps you feel like yourself again?

A. Quiet time where nobody needs anything from me.  
B. A good conversation with someone who energizes me.  
C. Doing something active or practical so I can reset physically.  
D. Switching into a creative or interesting project that absorbs me.

Reasoning: Separates social recharge from solitude without reducing introversion/extraversion to shyness. Options C and D catch people who recharge through activity or novelty rather than pure social/solo preference.

---

### Q02 — Information lens
**Purpose:** S/N, Openness, analysis vs practical evidence.

Prompt: When you first look at a new problem, what do you naturally notice first?

A. The concrete facts, constraints, and what can be verified.  
B. The pattern underneath it and where it may lead.  
C. The people affected and what they probably need.  
D. The fastest practical move that would create progress.

Reasoning: Measures sensing/intuitive style while also detecting people-orientation and action-orientation. Better than asking “details or big picture?” directly.

---

### Q03 — Decision standard
**Purpose:** T/F, Agreeableness, decision style.

Prompt: When a decision is difficult, what makes you trust the final choice most?

A. The logic is clean and consistent.  
B. The choice respects the people involved.  
C. The outcome is measurable and moves things forward.  
D. The decision still leaves room to adjust if new information appears.

Reasoning: Distinguishes logic, people-impact, outcome drive, and flexibility. This improves both MBTI-style and work-role interpretation.

---

### Q04 — Planning style
**Purpose:** J/P, Conscientiousness, autonomy, tolerance for ambiguity.

Prompt: A project is due in three weeks. Which approach feels most natural?

A. Build a clear plan early and follow it closely.  
B. Set a rough direction, then adapt as I learn more.  
C. Start with the hardest unknown and solve that first.  
D. Get the right people aligned before the work spreads out.

Reasoning: Gets beyond “organized vs spontaneous.” All options can be competent, but they reveal structure, adaptability, problem-first thinking, and collaboration-first instincts.

---

### Q05 — Communication processing
**Purpose:** E/I, T/F, workplace communication needs.

Prompt: In a serious conversation, when are you usually at your best?

A. After I have had time to think privately.  
B. While talking it through in real time.  
C. When the conversation stays direct and solution-focused.  
D. When the emotional context is acknowledged first.

Reasoning: Differentiates processing mode from emotional/analytical conversation style. Useful for team-chemistry and result language.

---

### Q06 — Novelty vs proven path
**Purpose:** Openness, S/N, risk/innovation career fit.

Prompt: If two paths could both work, which one pulls you more?

A. The proven path with clear evidence behind it.  
B. The original path that could become something better.  
C. The efficient path that gets a useful result fastest.  
D. The people-smart path that builds support as it goes.

Reasoning: Measures openness without asking “are you creative?” Also distinguishes pragmatic efficiency and coalition-building.

---

### Q07 — Closure preference
**Purpose:** J/P, Conscientiousness, execution style.

Prompt: What feels most satisfying at the end of a work session?

A. A finished checklist and a clear next step.  
B. A better understanding of the problem.  
C. A visible piece of progress, even if imperfect.  
D. A stronger connection or agreement with the people involved.

Reasoning: Reveals completion orientation, intellectual exploration, action bias, and relational progress.

---

### Q08 — Thinking depth
**Purpose:** N/T/I, analytical career fit, focus style.

Prompt: Which task would you choose if all paid the same and mattered equally?

A. Analyze a complex issue and explain the clean answer.  
B. Coordinate moving parts so the whole thing works smoothly.  
C. Persuade people around a useful idea or opportunity.  
D. Help someone solve a real problem directly.

Reasoning: Directly informs role match while also capturing personality style. This is one of the highest-value career-fit questions.

## Group B — Big Five backbone

### Q09 — Follow-through under friction
**Purpose:** Conscientiousness, DISC C/S, reliability.

Prompt: When a task becomes boring but still matters, what usually happens?

A. I finish it because reliability matters.  
B. I finish it if I can make the system cleaner or smarter.  
C. I push through if other people are counting on me.  
D. I look for a faster or more energizing way to get it done.

Reasoning: Measures follow-through without shaming lower-routine users. Distinguishes duty, systems improvement, service obligation, and efficiency-seeking.

---

### Q10 — Detail tolerance
**Purpose:** Conscientiousness, DISC C, operations/quality fit.

Prompt: How do you usually respond to small errors that could become bigger problems?

A. I want to catch and fix them early.  
B. I focus on the errors most likely to affect the outcome.  
C. I notice them, but I do not want them to slow everything down.  
D. I prefer someone detail-oriented to handle that part while I focus elsewhere.

Reasoning: Strong quality-control signal. Also prevents over-scoring everyone as detail-oriented by offering legitimate alternatives.

---

### Q11 — Curiosity driver
**Purpose:** Openness, learning style, creative/analysis fit.

Prompt: What kind of learning feels most alive to you?

A. Learning a practical skill I can use quickly.  
B. Understanding a deep idea or system.  
C. Exploring something creative, unusual, or future-facing.  
D. Learning through people, stories, and lived experience.

Reasoning: Splits practical, conceptual, creative, and social learning. Useful for recommendations and premium learning plans.

---

### Q12 — Social energy level
**Purpose:** Extraversion, DISC I, people-facing role fit.

Prompt: Which setting would likely bring out your best energy?

A. A lively room where ideas and reactions move fast.  
B. A small trusted group with thoughtful exchange.  
C. A quiet environment where I can focus deeply.  
D. A hands-on setting where I can move, test, or fix things.

Reasoning: More nuanced than introvert/extrovert. Distinguishes high social stimulation, selective social energy, deep focus, and physical/practical energy.

---

### Q13 — Cooperation style
**Purpose:** Agreeableness, DISC S/I, team behavior.

Prompt: In a group, what are people most likely to rely on you for?

A. Keeping the mood constructive and people included.  
B. Saying the direct thing others are avoiding.  
C. Making sure the work is accurate and thought through.  
D. Staying steady and helping people follow through.

Reasoning: All are valuable team contributions. Maps cleanly to DISC and Big Five agreeableness/conscientiousness patterns.

---

### Q14 — Conflict pattern
**Purpose:** Agreeableness, D/S/C, pressure behavior.

Prompt: When disagreement shows up, what is your first instinct?

A. Clarify the facts and remove confusion.  
B. Address it directly so the group can move.  
C. Lower the temperature and protect the relationship.  
D. Step back, think, and return when I can respond well.

Reasoning: Conflict is high-signal. This separates precision, dominance, harmony, and reflective processing.

---

### Q15 — Stress response
**Purpose:** Emotional stability / Neuroticism, pressure page, confidence.

Prompt: Under pressure, what tends to happen first?

A. I get sharper and more decisive.  
B. I become quieter and more analytical.  
C. I look for reassurance, alignment, or support.  
D. I feel scattered unless I can simplify the next step.

Reasoning: Measures pressure behavior without pathologizing. Useful for stress pattern, DISC, and result caveats.

---

### Q16 — Feedback response
**Purpose:** Emotional stability, growth style, agreeableness/conscientiousness.

Prompt: When you receive blunt feedback, what helps you use it best?

A. Specific examples and a clear standard.  
B. A respectful tone and evidence that the person gets me.  
C. Time alone to process before responding.  
D. A direct challenge that gives me something to prove.

Reasoning: Feedback style reveals defensiveness, motivation, and learning environment fit. It also improves practical result advice.

## Group C — DISC / work behavior

### Q17 — Team stuck moment
**Purpose:** DISC D/I/S/C, leadership style.

Prompt: A team is stuck and the deadline is getting close. What do you naturally do?

A. Make the call and get people moving.  
B. Rally the group and rebuild energy.  
C. Calm the room and create a steady path.  
D. Check the facts so the next move is correct.

Reasoning: Classic high-signal DISC scenario. Each option is competent but behaviorally distinct.

---

### Q18 — Influence style
**Purpose:** DISC I/D/C/S, communication and persuasion.

Prompt: When you need people to support an idea, what do you lean on most?

A. A compelling story that makes people care.  
B. A clear argument with evidence.  
C. A bold goal and visible momentum.  
D. Trust, patience, and one-on-one buy-in.

Reasoning: Separates charisma/story, logic/evidence, ambition/momentum, and relationship trust.

---

### Q19 — Pace preference
**Purpose:** DISC D/S/C/I, environment fit.

Prompt: Which pace usually helps you do your best work?

A. Fast pace, clear stakes, quick decisions.  
B. Steady pace, predictable expectations, low chaos.  
C. Deep pace, fewer interruptions, high accuracy.  
D. Varied pace, people contact, and visible momentum.

Reasoning: Direct environment-fit signal. Helps avoid recommending roles that match personality but violate preferred pace.

---

### Q20 — Standards vs momentum
**Purpose:** DISC C/D, conscientiousness, execution tradeoff.

Prompt: A project is good but not perfect. What is your likely move?

A. Ship it if it solves the real problem.  
B. Improve the weak spots before others see it.  
C. Get feedback from people who will use it.  
D. Reframe the goal if the current version misses the point.

Reasoning: Captures action bias, quality control, user/social feedback, and strategic reframing.

---

### Q21 — Responsibility style
**Purpose:** DISC, leadership/service/operations fit.

Prompt: Which responsibility would you rather own?

A. Making the final decision when the path is unclear.  
B. Keeping the process organized and dependable.  
C. Making sure people stay informed and engaged.  
D. Solving the hardest technical or conceptual problem.

Reasoning: Strong role-cluster question. Directly improves career recommendation quality.

## Group D — Career-fit vector

### Q22 — Work material preference
**Purpose:** People/data/things/ideas, RIASEC-like role fit.

Prompt: Which kind of work material do you most want in front of you every week?

A. Data, systems, research, or complex information.  
B. People, needs, relationships, or communication.  
C. Tools, products, operations, or physical/practical problems.  
D. Ideas, stories, designs, or future possibilities.

Reasoning: This is essential. Personality alone cannot produce strong career matches; work-material preference must be measured directly.

---

### Q23 — Energy over six months
**Purpose:** Sustainable motivation, career resonance.

Prompt: Which work would drain you least over six months?

A. Improving a system until it runs better.  
B. Helping people solve personal or practical problems.  
C. Building, fixing, or testing something tangible.  
D. Creating concepts, campaigns, content, or experiences.

Reasoning: Long-duration framing reduces fantasy answers. It asks what is sustainable, not just what sounds exciting.

---

### Q24 — Work environment preference
**Purpose:** remote/deep work/team/field/pace; role constraints.

Prompt: Which environment would probably help you perform best?

A. Quiet focus, clear ownership, minimal interruption.  
B. Collaborative energy, frequent conversation, shared momentum.  
C. Structured expectations, stable routines, reliable systems.  
D. Dynamic setting with variety, urgency, and real-time problem solving.

Reasoning: Career match improves dramatically when environment preference is included. This catches mismatches early.

---

### Q25 — Motivation driver
**Purpose:** values, career reward, Big Five/career vector.

Prompt: What makes work feel most worth it to you?

A. Mastering something difficult and becoming excellent.  
B. Seeing people benefit because I helped.  
C. Winning measurable outcomes or building momentum.  
D. Making something original, useful, or meaningful exist.

Reasoning: Motivation predicts role resonance better than personality labels alone. This question informs result tone and role paths.

---

### Q26 — Tradeoff tolerance
**Purpose:** career constraint, risk/autonomy/stability/social load.

Prompt: Which tradeoff are you most willing to accept for the right path?

A. More uncertainty if I get autonomy and upside.  
B. More routine if the work is stable and dependable.  
C. More social demand if the work has impact and connection.  
D. More complexity if the work is intellectually satisfying.

Reasoning: Great careers still contain friction. Asking tolerated friction is more predictive than asking ideal preferences.

## Group E — Calibration / confidence

### Q27 — Strength mirror
**Purpose:** consistency check across prior signals; result confidence.

Prompt: If someone described your strongest contribution, which would feel most accurate?

A. I bring clarity when things are confusing.  
B. I bring momentum when things are stuck.  
C. I bring steadiness when things are stressful.  
D. I bring originality when things feel stale.

Reasoning: This summarizes four broad contribution styles. It checks whether earlier MBTI/DISC/Big Five/career signals converge.

---

### Q28 — Anti-fit warning
**Purpose:** role mismatch protection; improves credibility.

Prompt: Which kind of work would become frustrating fastest?

A. Work with constant interruption and little time to think.  
B. Work with rigid rules and no room to improve anything.  
C. Work with heavy conflict, pressure, or emotional tension.  
D. Work with vague expectations and no clear standard for success.

Reasoning: This protects against bad role recommendations. Anti-fit questions often improve accuracy because people can identify draining environments more reliably than ideal ones.

## Recommended tie-breaker pool, not part of the fixed 28

These should only appear if the user has a close-call result.

1. E/I tie-breaker: When something important happens, do you understand it better by talking it out or thinking it through first?
2. T/F tie-breaker: If logic and human impact conflict, which one do you check last before deciding?
3. J/P tie-breaker: Does an unfinished open loop energize you with possibility or drain you with uncertainty?
4. DISC tie-breaker: Under deadline pressure, do you become more commanding, expressive, stabilizing, or exacting?
5. Role-fit tie-breaker: If pay and status were equal, would you choose analysis/systems, people/helping, building/operations, or creative/strategy?

## Why this database is stronger than the current 45-question flow

- Fewer weak lifestyle proxies.
- More direct measurement of work energy and career fit.
- Every item has an obvious scoring purpose.
- Four-option tradeoffs create better differentiation than binary questions.
- Positive-option framing lowers social desirability distortion.
- Anti-fit and pressure questions prevent generic flattering results.
- Calibration items support confidence-aware language.
